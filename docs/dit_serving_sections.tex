\section{Scope}
DitServe targets online mixtures of short image requests and long video requests.
The system under evaluation is the native C++ \texttt{cluster} executor and its
resident Wan and SD3.5 workers. Standalone model scripts are microbenchmarks,
not end-to-end serving results. The cost-model simulator is used only for
clearly labeled what-if studies and is never presented as a GPU measurement.

The evaluation uses the models present on the jjjkairos cluster:
Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B, Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, and SD3.5-Medium. Hardware, software, model
revisions, and parallel validity are recorded for every run. No result from a
Sana-based historical workload is used for this model combination.

\section{Background}
A diffusion Transformer (DiT) request performs repeated model predictions and
sampler updates before decoding the final latent~\cite{ho2020ddpm,rombach2022ldm,peebles2023dit}.
Wan uses a flow-matching model and UniPC sampler in our
configuration~\cite{wan2025}. With classifier-free guidance (CFG), each solver
step evaluates conditional and unconditional branches and combines them as
\begin{equation}
  v = v_u + g(v_c-v_u).
\end{equation}
Each branch contains a sequence of Transformer blocks. A request can therefore
be suspended at a solver boundary, between branches, or between blocks. These
boundaries differ in response time, retained state, and migration cost.

The serving objective is deadline-constrained goodput, not isolated generation
speed. End-to-end latency starts at request arrival and includes encoding,
queueing, execution, migration, and decoding. Late and failed requests remain
in the denominator and their resource consumption remains in the measurement
interval.

\section{Contribution Boundaries}
\subsection{Output-preserving execution}
The first mechanism is a block-level continuation for Wan. It retains the live
packet, branch position, solver state, and RNG so that a request can stop after
a Transformer block and resume without restarting the solver step. A
single-process block suspension is bit-identical to uninterrupted execution
with the same worker configuration. Cross-process continuation uses a
checksummed tensor packet and binds model, engine, dtype, and execution layout.
SP/CFG placement changes are permitted only at solver boundaries; an intra-step
SP packet cannot be moved to another group.

\paragraph{Definition of ``exact.''}
Exact is a comparison against uninterrupted execution on the same optimized
runtime, with identical model weights, dtypes, attention backend, sampler, seed,
and output decoder. It does not mean bit equality with an FP32-VAE pipeline,
with a different attention implementation, or across token-sharded GPU
reductions. CFG-parallel execution is tested separately for bitwise equality to
the same single-GPU execution. Sequence parallelism is not assumed to be
bitwise exact. BF16 VAE decoding and INT8 communication are separate numerical
modes and cannot be silently included in an exact common baseline.

\subsection{Placement-aware parallel forms}
The second mechanism selects among CFG parallelism, Ulysses sequence
parallelism, and their combinations using a placement-calibrated cost model.
CFG2 itself is available in xDiT~\cite{fang2024xdit}; the claimed systems
mechanism is its integration with sub-step scheduling, calibrated placement,
and communication-aware execution. Dual-stream branch and request interleaving
are evaluated by direct timing and communication-overlap measurements, not by
an assumed fixed discount.

For a form with CFG degree $c$, sequence degree $d$, compute term $A$,
communication volume $M$, collective latency $L$, and CFG exchange $E$, the
planning estimate has the form
\begin{equation}
  T(c,d)=\frac{A}{cd\eta}+T_{\mathrm{comm}}(M,c,d,B_d)+L(c,d)+\mathbf{1}[c>1]E.
\end{equation}
Bandwidth, latency, contention, and overlap are calibrated per placement.
Calibration and evaluation configurations are disjoint; per-request fudge
factors and bandwidth overrides inferred from the evaluation row are forbidden.
Held-out error is reported by model, shape, form, and placement. A measured
form table has precedence over this analytical estimate for forms calibrated
on the same placement; the analytical path is a fallback for unmeasured or
contended cases. Large held-out errors therefore limit, rather than promote,
the analytical contribution. A profile records the GPU UUIDs from its calibration
budget identity. Formal native runs require these UUIDs to match the current
allocation; otherwise the run is rejected or explicitly labelled as a diagnostic.
Approximate arms share one such calibration but retain independent run records.

\subsection{Quality-guarded approximation}
Late-step CFG folding and elastic subgroup fission are a separate approximate
mode. Folding skips the unconditional branch only after a configured minimum
number of full-CFG steps and reconstructs guidance from a stored CFG delta.
The online guard is evaluated against a fixed-step folding rule; neither is
presented as lossless. Taylor/feature caching and INT8 Ring communication are
optional independent ablations, not prerequisites for the primary mechanism.

Approximate results report quality distributions and failures across prompts
and seeds. A single sample or a PSNR threshold on one video does not establish
a quality guarantee. Exact and approximate arms have separate tables and are
never mixed into one unlabeled ranking.

\subsection{Engineering baseline}
CPU text encoding, prompt embedding caches, Flash attention, allocator cache
release, chunked VAE decode, fixed offline image batching, and the C++ process
supervisor are engineering components. Where supported by every arm, they are
enabled uniformly; otherwise their absence or presence is explicit. They are
not counted as new scheduling theory. Rank-0 asynchronous VAE overlap is
claimed only when the native C++ controller actually overlaps and measures it.

\section{Execution and Evidence Contract}
A valid end-to-end result must contain:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the native run record, final \texttt{REPORT.md} and
        \texttt{results.xlsx}, complete configuration, and failure list;
  \item \texttt{BUILD_INFO.json}, core hash, image digest, model revisions,
        GPU names/UUIDs, topology, and node placement;
  \item per-request arrival, start, finish, deadline, execution form, quanta,
        migration, and output status;
  \item an exact/approximate label and the precise numerical baseline;
  \item separate real, microbenchmark, and simulated metrics.
\end{enumerate}
A policy name in the planner is insufficient evidence that the worker executed
the mechanism. The returned worker execution record must match the planned
form and approximation flags. Approximation flags that are not implemented by
the worker fail closed rather than being ignored.

Baselines are re-implemented on the same native executor, workers, calibrated
profile and trace, and are named by provenance. Methods available only as
preprints are named by method rather than by system; the published TetriServe
and the xDiT engine keep their names with the re-implementation scope stated.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{xDiT}~\cite{fang2024xdit}: the usual deployment of the engine:
        each model on its own GPUs (the image share fixed in advance from the
        trace's expected GPU time), videos on a fixed CFG pair, FCFS, no
        preemption.
  \item \textbf{TetriServe (re-impl.)}~\cite{lu2026tetriserve}: step-level
        sequence parallelism re-planned every round by a deadline group knapsack
        over minimal feasible allocations. Rounds are approximated by a fixed
        number of solver steps; its batching and multi-node deployment are not
        reproduced.
  \item \textbf{SBP-ESP}, the method of GenServe~\cite{ye2026genserve}:
        step-boundary video preemption, elastic SP re-planned every step by an
        SLO-aware knapsack, dynamic image batching and single-GPU VAE decode;
        its exact utility function is not reproduced.
  \item \textbf{EDF-BF}, the \texttt{EdfBestFit} policy of
        GF-DiT~\cite{gfdit2026}: smallest size meeting the deadline, otherwise
        the largest that fits, then EDF surplus redistribution. Its
        group-free collectives are vendored and evaluated separately.
\end{itemize}
Simple heuristics are \textbf{EDF-CFG2} (every video on one CFG pair) and
\textbf{CFG2-First} (DitServe's form rules without sub-step preemption). The
planner of an earlier DitServe version, a hand-weighted knapsack, overlaps with
TetriServe's formulation and is reported only as an ablation. Ablations remove
one DitServe mechanism at a time: block preemption, dominated-form pruning,
CFG-pair admission and pair-fit image placement.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\paragraph{Hardware and models.}
The physical cells are one eight-GPU node and two eight-GPU nodes on
jjjkairos. GPU model, memory, PCIe/NVLink topology, and cross-node networking
are measured in the job. The main model pairs are Wan2.1+SD3.5 and
Wan2.2+SD3.5. Short development shapes and final serving shapes are reported
separately; no shape is selected post hoc to hide negative scaling.

\paragraph{Workloads.}
Real runs use frozen public-prompt traces with 20/80 and 50/50 video/image
request mixes, Poisson and burst/Gamma arrivals, multiple offered loads, and
multiple SLO scales. Development pilots establish capacity and detect failures;
they are not headline results. Final cells use at least three seeds and enough
requests to avoid single-request or single-wave conclusions. All policies see
the same trace, model configuration, warmup, and output mode.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
We report per-modality SLO attainment, p50/p95/p99 latency,
deadline-constrained goodput, makespan, and GPU-seconds per completed request.
The 16-GPU study reports both a single distributed job and two independent
8-GPU jobs; weak scaling across nodes is not conflated with single-request
speedup.

\paragraph{Mechanism studies.}
Block preemption reports stop-latency CDFs, resident state size, transfer
bytes/time, steady-state overhead, and end-to-end ablation. A controlled
\texttt{edf-cfg2} versus \texttt{block-cfg2} arm holds the parallel form
fixed and compares digest-bound outputs. Doorbell-to-safe-point stop time is
reported separately from request queueing and from the count of requests that
actually preempt a video. Parallel forms report direct latency, numerical
comparison, and communication overlap. The cost model is fitted on one
partition and evaluated on held-out models, shapes, forms, and placements.
Simulator sweeps beyond measured cells are labeled extrapolations and include
validation against native runs.

\paragraph{Quality study.}
Each approximate video arm is evaluated on at least 32 prompts and three
seeds per model, with exact references from the same optimized runtime.
PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and VBench dimensions are reported as distributions with
failure counts. Fixed and curvature-gated folding share the same late-step
window, predictor damping and velocity coefficient, and per-request fold
budget. The curvature guard must beat fixed-step folding at comparable
compute or quality to remain a contribution. If it does not, the paper retains
only the simpler mechanism or drops the approximation claim.
